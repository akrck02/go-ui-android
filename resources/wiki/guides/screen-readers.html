🗣️ Screen readers

Every piece reads under TalkBack as Android’s own do: a finger moved over the screen reads what is under it (“Lamp, switch, on”), a swipe goes to the next thing, a double tap presses it, and the thing read is ringed. Go UI works the list out from the boxes (its docs): a control is read by the texts inside it, texts side by side are read together, a list holds its rows, and what is behind a dialog or a sheet is left out. The pieces add what the boxes cannot tell:

Piece Read as
Switch, Checkbox, RadioButton A switch, a checkbox, a radio button, and whether it is on
SegmentedButtons, ButtonGroup, a dropdown’s choices, the date picker’s days The chosen one selected
NavigationBar, NavigationRail, Tabs Tabs, the one shown selected; a badge after the label
TopAppBar, Dialog The title as a heading; the arrow as “Back” (or “Close”)
TextField The label over the box names the field, read with what is typed (not a secret one’s); the screen reader’s own typing, pasting and dictation fill it
Slider Its range; the volume keys or a swipe up or down move it a step
FloatingMenu, FloatingToolbar The menu’s Label (“Create”), “Close” while open; each tool’s label
Icons with no label (a field’s cross, the month’s arrows, a Menu’s button, the split button’s arrow) What they do (“More options”): Look.Words, English by default (EnglishWords)

An icon button of the program’s own reads its icon’s name (“share 2”) unless it has one: NamedIconButton("share-2", "Share"), or Describe after any piece. The gallery’s test goes to every screen with double taps alone, and checks that every control has a name.

The package carries one small Java class for it (go-ui-apk compiles it in, see packaging). It was tried on the Android 14 emulator with an accessibility service acting as TalkBack does: the gallery’s tree reaches the system with every name, role and state, and a click goes to another destination, the focus rings a checkbox, a second click ticks it, a scroll moves the list on and text set on a field (“North light 🌊”) lands in it whole. TalkBack itself, on a phone, is not tried yet.
