📱 Feeling at home on a phone

What a phone does that a desktop does not: fingers that swipe and hold, the back gesture, a screen that turns, notifications, and the phone’s own services. Most of it comes from Go UI and Go Renderer; the pieces use it the way Android’s own applications do.

Gestures

Go UI follows the one finger on a touch screen from where it comes down. Once it has moved a few pixels, the way it went first decides who takes it: the innermost list for up and down, the innermost of the sideways pieces (a carousel inside swiping pages) for sideways. The control it came down on is then not touched, and a finger that hardly moves stays a touch. So the pieces sit inside each other freely: the gallery has a carousel inside a list inside swiping pages, and rows swiped away inside a list pulled to refresh.

  • Long press. A finger held still on a piece for half a second (Go UI’s LongPressed; with a mouse, its right button) long-presses it, and lifting it then is no touch. look.LongPressed() after a piece tells, and gives the phone’s short buzz, as Android’s own applications do. A list starts choosing with it: the row held is chosen (Item.Selected), touches choose more, and the top bar says how many, with a cross to stop (TopBar.Close) and actions for what is chosen.
  touched := look.ListItem("row", material.Item{Headline: light.name, Selected: light.chosen})
  switch {
  case look.LongPressed():
  	light.chosen = true
  case touched && choosing:
  	light.chosen = !light.chosen
  }
  • Pull to refresh. The indicator follows the pull (Go UI’s ScrollPull); let go past 64 pixels, PullToRefresh returns true, and the program refreshes, keeping refreshing set until done.
  • Swiping pages. A page let go past a third of the way, or flicked, turns to the next; the tab underline moves with the pages, between two tabs while a page is half way.
  • Rows swiped away. Past two fifths of their width, or flicked, they go; short of that they slide back.
  • Carousels. Let go, they glide on with the finger’s speed and settle at a tile’s edge.
  • The stick. The finger that comes down on it holds it; Forward and Sideways go from -1 to 1. Other fingers stay free, so a program can turn a view with them (context.Touches(), leaving out stick.Touching(touch)). It can sit anywhere, in a Push scope, a list or swiping pages: the finger steering it is its own, so the pages and the list stay still under it.

Back and motion

A phone’s back gesture reaches the program. Dialogs, sheets, the drawer and the floating menu answer it first and close; then look.Back() tells the screen, which stops choosing rows or goes back (to the first destination, to the list a detail came from). A back nobody answers sends the program to the background, as Android’s own applications do at their first screen. Ask for it after drawing what goes over the screen, so that gets it first:

defer func() {
	if screen != firstScreen && look.Back() {
		screen = firstScreen
	}
}()

Pieces move as Material’s do, and only while they move does the window draw: the switch’s handle slides and grows, the navigation bar’s pill widens under the chosen icon, the tabs’ underline slides, dialogs rise in as the screen darkens, sheets and the drawer slide in, the snackbar slides up, the floating menu’s actions rise one after another, and look.Enter(key, …) lets a screen rise into place when it is first shown.

They leave with motion too, though a program simply stops drawing them: a dialog answered fades out, a sheet slides back down, the drawer slides back off the side, the snackbar fades when its time is up, a side sheet slides back off its side, a floating menu’s actions sink and fade as it closes, and a row’s more folds back under it. Go UI keeps what such a layer painted last (LayerOptions.Leave) and lets that picture go; nothing in it can be touched on its way out.

Turning the phone

A phone on its side has more width than height to spare, and its camera’s cutout at a side. Material moves the destinations from a bar at the bottom to a rail down the left:

if look.Wide() {
	context.Box(ui.Layout{Width: ui.Grow(), Height: ui.Grow()}, func() {
		look.NavigationRail(places, &screen)
		look.Body(true, func() { drawScreen(look) })
	})
	return
}
context.Box(ui.Layout{Direction: ui.Vertical, Width: ui.Grow(), Height: ui.Grow()}, func() {
	look.Body(false, func() { drawScreen(look) })
	look.NavigationBar(places, &screen)
})

Body keeps the content clear of the cutout; go-ui-apk’s window theme paints beside it (shortEdges), so no black strip is left there.

Notifications

A program’s notifications go through Go UI’s window: window.Notify(id, title, text) shows one in the phone’s shade, even while the program is in the background, and touching it opens the program. Android 13 and later show them only once the person allows it: check window.NotificationsAllowed() and ask with window.AskForNotifications() when they do something that needs them. Build the package with go-ui-apk -notifications, which names the permission. In a browser they are the page’s notifications; on the desktop they are not shown yet.

The phone’s services

What a program asks of the phone goes through Go UI’s window, and works in a browser too:

  • Links and sharing. window.OpenLink(link) opens a web page in the browser, mailto: in the mail, tel: in the phone; window.Share(title, text) offers a text through the system’s sheet. The gallery shares the lighthouses chosen in its list, and opens its project page from the menu.
  • The clipboard. look.Context.Copy(text) puts a text on the phone’s clipboard, and text fields copy and paste through it.
  • Preferences. window.Preference(name) and window.KeepPreference(name, value) keep small values between runs. Android may end a program in the background without a word, so keep them in Options.Hidden, called when the program leaves the screen: the gallery keeps its screen, its theme and its colours there, and comes back as it was left.
  • Sound. The feedback screen plays a note through Go Sound, which goes quiet when the gallery leaves the screen (Options.Hidden) and plays again when it comes back (Options.Shown). go-ui-apk passes the NDK’s C++ compiler too, for the sound output’s C++ (Oboe).
  • The person’s text size. Text grows with the phone’s font size setting; icons and paddings keep theirs, as in Android’s own applications.
