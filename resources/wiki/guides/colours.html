🎨 Colours

LightDark

A Scheme holds Material’s colour roles: the primary colour and the text on it, its container, the surface and the raised containers over it, the outlines, the error colours and the inverse ones.

  • material.System(dark) takes the colours the phone made from its wallpaper (Android 12 and later, Material You), as the system’s own applications do, and Material’s baseline elsewhere (an older phone, the desktop, a browser). Call it in every frame: the colours change with the wallpaper. The roles are taken from Android’s palettes as its own dynamic colours take them: the primary from the first accent palette, the containers from the second, the surfaces from the neutral ones; the error colours stay Material’s.
  • material.Baseline(dark) is Material’s own purple scheme, always the same.
  • material.FromColour(colour, dark) makes a whole scheme from one colour (a brand’s, a place’s), as Material’s own tools do: palettes of its hue at every tone, as Android makes them from a wallpaper, then the roles from them. Each tone is the colour’s CIELAB lightness, as in Material, so text keeps its contrast whatever the colour; the hues are worked in OKLCH, where they stay even. PalettesFromColour gives the palettes themselves.
  • scheme.WithPrimary(…) puts a brand’s colour in the primary roles only.
  • scheme.Theme(dark) gives go-ui’s own controls (its slider, text fields, menus) the scheme and sizes for fingers; set it as the context’s theme before the pieces.
